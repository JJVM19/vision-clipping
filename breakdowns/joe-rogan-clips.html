The Joe Rogan Clip Economy: Half of the Biggest Podcast’s Views Are Clips

August 2026 · Vision Clipping · A cited breakdown of the discovery engine behind podcasting’s king
Summary
  • The official JRE Clips channel holds 6.07 billion lifetime views against 6.64 billion on the main channel: roughly half of all official Rogan YouTube views ever are clips, before counting clips on the main channel itself.
  • When Spotify bought the show for a reported $100M+ in 2020, the clips were deliberately left out of the exclusive. Rogan’s own words: full episodes go, “we will still have clips up on YouTube”.
  • The 2024 Trump episode showed what the machine does at full power: 26M views in 24 hours, more than the combined average prime-time audiences of six major TV networks within days.

In 2020, Spotify paid a reported nine figures to make The Joe Rogan Experience exclusive, and Rogan carved one thing out of the deal: the clips. Full episodes disappeared behind Spotify’s wall for three years. The clips never left YouTube for a day. That single carve-out is the clearest statement any deal has ever made about where a show’s growth actually comes from.

Half the views are clips, by Rogan’s own numbers

The arithmetic sits in public view on two channel pages. The main channel, PowerfulJRE, holds 6.64 billion lifetime views across 3,600+ videos. The dedicated JRE Clips channel holds 6.07 billion across 6,000+ clips, posted at a steady one to three a day since 2017. Roughly half of every official Rogan view on YouTube, ever, came through the clips channel alone, and that is before counting the clips and Shorts that also live on the main channel.

The carve-out that named the strategy

The 2020 Spotify deal was reported by the Wall Street Journal at more than $100 million, later reported by the New York Times at $200 million-plus. Announcing it, Rogan spelled out the exception himself: “We will still have clips up on YouTube but full versions of the show will only be on Spotify after the end of the year.”

Read that as a distribution decision: the product could move behind a wall, the discovery engine could not. For three years, clips were the only Rogan on the world’s biggest video platform, feeding the funnel for a show people had to leave YouTube to hear. When the deal was renewed in 2024, at a reported $250 million, exclusivity was dropped entirely. Spotify concluded what the clips had been proving all along: reach beats walls. The show has since ranked No. 1 on Spotify Wrapped globally for 2025 and topped YouTube’s inaugural weekly podcast chart, a chart that, fittingly, excludes clips from its own counting.

What the machine does at full power

October 2024 was the stress test. The Trump episode did 26 million YouTube views in its first 24 hours, 38 million within four days, and sits above 62 million today. Newsweek ran the comparison that ended the podcasts-versus-TV argument: at 33 million views the episode had already beaten the combined average prime-time audiences of ABC, NBC, CBS, Fox News, MSNBC and CNN, roughly 22.7 million between them. And around the episode swarmed the usual second economy: cutdowns, Shorts and reaction clips carrying the moments to feeds the full three hours would never reach.

What everyone below Rogan should take from it

Rogan can afford to treat clips casually; a show with 21 million subscribers gets clipped whether it likes it or not, and even the third-party clip scene around JRE survives mostly as a long tail of small accounts. Everyone else has to do deliberately what happens to him automatically. The lesson in his numbers is structural: the clips channel is not a byproduct of the show, it is half the audience. The show that treats clips as an afterthought is leaving its discovery engine unbuilt.

Building that engine deliberately means what it has meant all series long: cut daily, post to channels you own, aim at the audience that converts to listeners, and measure it.
