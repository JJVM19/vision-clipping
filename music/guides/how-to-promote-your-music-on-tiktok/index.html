How to promote your music on TikTok in 2026

October 2026 · Vision Clipping · A step by step guide for artists and labels
Summary
  • TikTok is where songs break: a TikTok-commissioned Luminate study found 84% of 2024’s new Billboard Global 200 entries went viral on TikTok first.
  • Your own posts build the artist. Other people’s videos break the song: creators, fan edits and clips using your official sound.
  • The measure that matters is creations, the number of videos using your sound. Views without creations mean people like the edit, not the song.

Promoting music on TikTok is less about your own account than most artists think. Your posts matter, but songs break when other people use them: in their videos, their edits, their memes. This guide covers both halves, in the order to do them.

Why TikTok matters for every release

The platform’s own figures are hard to ignore. A TikTok-commissioned Luminate study found 84% of songs that entered the Billboard Global 200 in 2024 went viral on TikTok first. TikTok says 8 of the 10 Billboard number ones in 2025 and all 13 UK number ones that year had a viral moment on the app. And its Add to Music App button has been used to save over 3 billion songs to streaming services. These are TikTok’s numbers, so read them as the platform making its case, but no other channel comes close.

Step 1: Set up the sound before anything else

  • Deliver the track through your distributor so it appears in TikTok’s sound library with your name and artwork.
  • Find your official sound page and use it on every post you make. Ask everyone who posts with your song to do the same.
  • Use the right section. When you add the song, start it on the strongest ten seconds, not the intro.

Step 2: Post from your own account, daily, around one song

Your account is where people go after a clip makes them curious. Give them something to land on:

  • Snippets before release to test which section people react to.
  • The story of the song: where it came from, the studio, the line everyone asks about.
  • Performances and reactions: live versions, fans singing it back, first listens.
  • Formats people can copy: a dance, a transition, a caption trend. Anything that invites a duet or a stitch.

Keep the focus on one song for weeks. Switching songs every few days resets the familiarity you were building.

Step 3: Give people versions to use

Sped-up, slowed and instrumental versions are now standard, because fans kept making them and they kept charting. RAYE’s “Escapism” reached UK number one as an independent release after sped-up remixes spread across TikTok and she released official ones. Put every version on your official sound so the counts add up in one place.

Step 4: Get other people posting it

Creators

Paying creators to use a song costs from $25 for micro creators to about $10,000 for top stars, per Billboard. It can start a trend, but one post is one moment, and paid posts have to be disclosed as advertising.

Fan pages and edit pages

Labels run or work with networks of pages that post edits with the song in them. Billboard reported that bbno$’s team had close to 60 fan pages posting one to four times a day for “It Boy”. Pages like these put the song under content people already want to watch, without asking them to care about the artist first.

Clipping

Clipping is the scaled version of edit pages: editors cut short videos with your song in them and post them across many accounts every day. It is the cheapest reach available, at roughly a dollar per thousand views in a typical campaign according to Vulture, and it is what labels now use to keep a song in feeds for weeks.

Step 5: Track creations, saves and streams

Views tell you an edit was watched. These tell you the song is catching:

  • Creations on your sound: how many videos use it. This is the number TikTok and the charts care about.
  • Saves and Add to Music App: people keeping the song.
  • Daily streams: whether listening climbs week on week.

If views are high but creations are flat, the edits are working and the hook is not. Try a different section of the song before spending more.

Common mistakes

  • Posting the intro instead of the hook.
  • Re-uploading the song instead of using the official sound, so views never reach your page.
  • Buying views or followers. They do nothing for discovery and put the account at risk.
  • Judging a push after one week.

Frequently asked questions

How do I promote my music on TikTok?

Deliver the track so it has an official sound, use that sound on every post, post daily from your own account around one song, release sped-up and instrumental versions, then get other people posting it through creators, edit pages or clipping. Measure creations on the sound, saves and streams.

What is the best way to promote a song on TikTok?

Get the song into other people’s videos. Your own posts build interest, but songs break when creators, fan pages and edits use the sound. Clipping does this at scale, putting the song under football, culture and comp edits posted daily across many pages.

How do I promote my music on TikTok without showing my face?

Edits and clips do not need you on camera. Put the song under football, culture, ranked or comp edits, post lyric and visualiser clips, and work with edit pages or a clipping operation that posts with your official sound.

How much does it cost to promote music on TikTok?

TikTok ads start from a $50 campaign budget. Creators charge from $25 to about $10,000 a post. Clipping campaigns typically cost about $1 per 1,000 views, and managed packages like ours start at £1,000 a month.
