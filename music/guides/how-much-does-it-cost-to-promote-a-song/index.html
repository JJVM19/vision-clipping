How much does it cost to promote a song in 2026?

October 2026 · Vision Clipping · Sourced prices for every way to push a song
Summary
  • Paying creators to use a song runs from $25 a post for micro creators to about $10,000 for a top TikTok star, per Billboard. Agencies put a worthwhile creator campaign at $5,000 and up.
  • Clipping is the cheapest reach in the market: clients typically pay about $1 per thousand views, against roughly $10 for a billboard and $30 or more for TV, according to Vulture.
  • What you are really buying is not views but repetition. Budget for a month or more of daily posting on one song, not one big post.

Ask five people what it costs to promote a song and you will get five answers, because they are pricing five different things. Here is what each route actually costs in 2026, from people who have seen the invoices, and what each one buys you.

How much does TikTok promotion cost?

The cheapest way in is TikTok’s own ad platform. A campaign budget must exceed $50, with ad groups from $20 a day. One ad analytics firm puts the average TikTok ads CPM at $4.80, though that figure comes from ecommerce brands, not music. The catch for songs is the label: an ad is marked “Sponsored”, people scroll past adverts, and a paid post rarely makes anyone use your sound. Vulture reports a sponsored TikTok ad can cost ten times what a clipping campaign does for the same reach.

How much do TikTok influencers charge to use a song?

This is the route most labels have used for years. Billboard’s reporting puts the range at $25 for a micro creator to $10,000 for a TikTok star, with top creators rarely fetching more than $10,000 for a song. NPR spoke to music influencers in 2026: one with 80,000 followers charges $150 to $400 per post, another with 1.1 million charges about $300 to $600. Two marketing agencies told Billboard that $5,000 is the low end for a campaign worth running, and spend can reach $80,000.

Below the creators sit music and meme pages. Empire told Billboard it can “make a splash” with 400 or 500 bucks on a curator post, and a rap blog quoted $1,000 for a solo post. And at the very bottom of the ladder, one label boss told Billboard he would rather pay a kid $50 to make six edits for a song than pay an influencer for one post.

How much does a music clipping campaign cost?

Clipping pays for distribution rather than personality: editors cut short videos with your song in them and post across many accounts. Variety reports that music clipping campaigns run on average $1,000 to $5,000, paying clippers $1 to $5 for every 1,000 impressions until the budget runs out. From the client’s side, Vulture puts a typical campaign at roughly a dollar per thousand views, and MIDiA Research says many campaigns cost as little as $1 CPM.

How to spend a song promotion budget

  • Under £1,000. Make the versions editors want (sped-up, slowed, instrumental), post daily edits yourself, and put a few hundred into meme or curator pages that suit the song.
  • £1,000 to £3,000 a month. Daily clipping across pages that already have an audience, focused on one song and two or three formats, for at least a month.
  • £5,000 and up. Clipping plus accounts you own, so the audience you build stays with you, with a handful of creator posts around the moments that matter.

Whatever the budget, put it behind one song for long enough to work. A single big post is a moment. Repetition is what turns a hook into a hit.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to promote a song on TikTok?

TikTok ads start from a $50 campaign budget and average around $4.80 per 1,000 impressions (ecommerce benchmark). Paying creators runs from $25 a post for micro creators to about $10,000 for top stars. Clipping campaigns typically cost the client about $1 per 1,000 views.

How much do influencers charge to promote a song?

NPR found mid-size music influencers charging $150 to $600 a post in 2026. Billboard reported a range of $25 for micro creators to about $10,000 for TikTok stars, with full creator campaigns from $5,000 to $80,000.

How much does a music clipping campaign cost?

Variety reports music clipping campaigns averaging $1,000 to $5,000, paying clippers $1 to $5 per 1,000 impressions. Managed operations charge monthly instead; ours start at £1,000 a month for 90 posts.

What is the cheapest way to promote a song?

Per view, clipping is the cheapest paid reach, at around $1 per thousand views. The cheapest overall is doing it yourself: releasing sped-up and instrumental versions, and posting daily edits with your official sound attached.
