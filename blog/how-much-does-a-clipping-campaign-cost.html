How Much Does a Clipping Campaign Cost?

August 2026 · Vision Clipping
Summary
  • Most clipping campaigns are priced per view, roughly $0.50 to $25 per 1,000 views depending on the niche.
  • A real campaign budget usually starts around $5,000 to $70,000+, set by the total views you want to buy.
  • The per-view sticker price hides the real costs: botting, no ownership, and no attribution.
  • Budget for cost per real result and a channel you keep, not the cheapest thousand views.

A clipping campaign is not priced like a normal marketing project. You do not pay for hours or deliverables, you pay for views, which makes the total budget the number that actually matters. Here is what a campaign costs in 2026, what sets the figure, and the costs that never show up on the invoice.

The unit: cost per thousand views

Open clipping campaigns pay a crowd of creators per view. Forbes put the going rate at roughly $1 to $5 per thousand views for brand campaigns, while Yahoo Finance reported a wider band of about 50 cents to $25 per thousand depending on the niche. NPR gave concrete examples in its report on the clipping economy: $1 per thousand for sports clips, $25 per thousand for an AI startup, and one prediction market paying 50 cents per thousand against a total budget of $70,000.

A $5,000 influencer post buys roughly the same reach as 500 individual clips.

What a campaign budget looks like

Because you buy views, the budget is set by how many views you want and the per-view rate for your niche. A small test might be a few thousand dollars; a serious brand push runs from around $5,000 into the tens of thousands. The Polymarket example NPR cited, a $70,000 budget at 50 cents per thousand, buys on the order of 140 million views. The maths is simple. The judgement is not.

Campaign sizeTypical budgetWhat it buys
Test$1,000–$5,000A first read on whether the content clips well
Growth push$5,000–$25,000Millions of views across a wave of clips
Scaled campaign$25,000–$70,000+Tens of millions of views, sustained

The costs that are not on the invoice

The per-view price is not the true cost. Three things quietly eat the value of a cheap campaign. First, paying per view is a direct incentive to inflate views, so a share of what you buy can be botted or gamed. Second, you do not own the accounts, so the audience is not yours to keep once the campaign ends. Third, most campaigns hand you a screenshot of view counts and nothing else, so you cannot tell which clips drove real results. For the wider picture, see how much a clipping agency costs and whether clipping is a scam.

Budgeting for results, not views

The right question is not "what is the cheapest thousand views," it is "what is the cost per real result, and do I own the channel at the end." A managed campaign on accounts you own costs more per view than an anonymous bounty, and usually costs less per customer, because the reach is real, targeted, and measured. Frequently asked questions

How much does a clipping campaign cost?

Clipping campaigns are priced per view, roughly $0.50 to $25 per 1,000 views depending on the niche, so the total budget is what matters. Real campaigns typically run from around $5,000 into the tens of thousands, and one campaign NPR cited had a $70,000 budget.

How is a clipping campaign priced?

Almost always per thousand views. You set a total budget and a per-view rate, and the campaign buys views until the budget is spent. Forbes put brand rates at about $1 to $5 per thousand views.

Is a cheap clipping campaign worth it?

Not always. A low per-view price often hides botted views, no account ownership and no attribution, so cheap reach you cannot measure or keep rarely turns into results. Judge on cost per real result, not the per-view sticker.
