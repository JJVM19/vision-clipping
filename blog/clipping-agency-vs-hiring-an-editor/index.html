Clipping Agency vs Hiring an Editor: Which Is Right for You?

Last updated July 2026 · Vision Clipping
Summary
  • An editor gives you clips only, cheaper per clip, but you run the accounts, posting and strategy.
  • A clipping agency runs the whole operation (edit + accounts + posting + attribution) for a monthly retainer.
  • Hire an editor to test a channel; hire an agency when distribution is a core growth lever.
  • Per clip the editor wins; per real result the agency often wins once your own time is counted.

It looks like a cost question, an editor is a few dollars a clip, an agency is a monthly retainer, but it’s really a question of what you want to own and how much of the work you want to do yourself. Here’s the honest side-by-side, and a simple way to tell which one fits where you are.

The short answer

Hire an editor if you need clips and are happy to run the accounts, posting and strategy yourself. Hire a clipping agency if you want distribution as an outcome, a team that edits, runs accounts you own, posts at scale across markets, and reports on what worked, without you managing any of it.

What an editor actually gives you

An editor turns your long-form content into good clips. That’s it, and for one channel, that can be plenty. What they don’t do: open and warm accounts, post on a consistent cadence across platforms, target specific markets, or tell you which clip drove a signup. Those jobs stay with you, and at volume they quietly become a full-time operations role.

What a clipping agency actually gives you

A managed agency runs the whole loop: editing, accounts, posting cadence, geo-targeted distribution, and attribution. The good ones run accounts you own, so the audience is yours to keep. You’re paying for the operation and the management, the part that turns clips into a distribution channel instead of a folder of videos.

The cost reality

Per clip, the editor always wins. But the sticker price hides the real cost: the accounts, the posting time, the management, and the fact that with no attribution you’re flying blind. Add it up and the do-it-yourself route often costs more than a retainer once your own hours are in the maths. We break the numbers down in how much a clipping agency costs.

When an editor is the right call

  • You’re testing whether a channel works at all.
  • You have the time and systems to run accounts and posting yourself.
  • You only need a handful of clips a week on one platform.

When an agency is the right call

  • Distribution is a core growth lever, not an experiment.
  • Running accounts and posting has become your bottleneck.
  • You want reach into specific markets and attribution you can act on.
  • You’d rather own a channel than manage a team.

Frequently asked questions

Is a clipping agency better than hiring an editor?

It depends on your goal. An editor is better if you just need clips and will run the accounts, posting and strategy yourself. A clipping agency is better if you want distribution as an outcome, a managed team running accounts you own, posting at scale with attribution, without doing the operations yourself.

How much does an editor cost compared to a clipping agency?

A freelance editor typically charges $15 to $150 per clip. A managed agency runs roughly $2,000 to $15,000+ per month because it includes accounts, posting, geo-targeting, management and reporting. Per clip the editor is cheaper; per real result the agency is often cheaper once your own time is counted.

Can I start with an editor and move to an agency later?

Yes, and many founders do. Starting with an editor is a sensible way to test a channel. Once it works and running it yourself becomes the bottleneck, moving to a managed agency lets you scale distribution without hiring and managing a team.
